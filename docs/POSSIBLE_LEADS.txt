POSSIBLE LEADS: things we could not settle, that you might be able to
=====================================================================

This project reverse-engineered the RODECaster Pro 2 and Duo control protocol from one
Pro 2 and one Duo, on two Windows PCs, in one building. A lot of what is left open is
open only because of that: no second unit, no SD card, no wireless transmitter, nobody
to listen at the right moment.

If you have this repo and a desk, several of these are minutes of work for you and
impossible for us. Every one below is written so you can run it without reading the rest
of the project first. Findings are welcome; so are negatives, which are worth as much.

Read `RODECASTER_PROTOCOL.txt` for the byte-level protocol and `docs/PROJECT_GUIDE.md`
part 2 for the invariants before writing anything to a desk.


BEFORE YOU WRITE ANYTHING TO A DESK
-----------------------------------

**Never sweep properties.** The desk publishes `powerOffRequest`,
`updateInitiateRequested`, `updateRebootRequested`, `updateResetDeviceRequested` and
`disableAllPhysicalButtons`. "Write each one and read it back" would reboot the unit,
start a firmware update, or lock its front panel. `updateRebootRequested` is confirmed
to reboot the desk.

**Never write on an unconfirmed selector.** Selectors are reused across regions, so a
wrong address does not politely miss: the record lands on some object anyway, and can
append a STRAY node. A stray reads back exactly like a real property, accepts writes,
and does nothing - and it is only cleared by a factory reset. One of the leads below
exists because a stray silently invalidated a whole session's results.

**A factory reset can take the unit off the USB bus**, about one time in five on the
firmware we tested (1.7.3). It is a fault in the unit rather than in any host software,
it does not recover by replugging USB, and the power lead has to come out. Back up first
and expect it.


LEAD 1: Bluetooth scanning works on a Duo and not on a Pro 2. Is that every Pro 2?
---------------------------------------------------------------------------------

**What we found.** Writing `btDoScan` = true on a RODECaster Duo fills `btScan1` with
`'<MAC address> - <device name>'` for a discoverable device in range. On a Pro 2, the
same write with the same code returns nothing: a Pixel phone on its Bluetooth screen, a
fresh false-to-true transition, sixty seconds, no slot filled.

**Both easy explanations are dead.** The Pro 2's radio is alive - a phone paired to it
and played audio through it minutes before the test. And it does publish Bluetooth state
into the model - `btConnectedAddress` read back the phone's address while connected.

**What we cannot tell.** Whether that is our particular Pro 2 or every Pro 2. One unit
cannot answer it.

**What to run.** Write `btDoScan` = true on the NETWORK block with something
unambiguously discoverable nearby - an Android phone on its Bluetooth settings screen is
the most reliable target. Wait up to a minute, then read `btScan1` through `btScan10`.
Report either result.

**Why it matters.** Bluetooth pairing on these desks is touchscreen-only, so a blind
owner cannot do it at all. A working scan is the way in to an accessible pairing flow.
The rest of the family - `btDoPair`, `btPairCode`, `btDoConnect`, `btDoDisconnect`,
`btDoUnPair` - has never been tested on either desk and must not be assumed dead because
the scan looked dead.

**Two things to know before you start.** The flag does NOT clear itself, so write it
back to false when you are done, and toggle it false before true to force a fresh
transition. And `btPairedNumber1` through `5` stay empty even with a phone genuinely
paired, so do not use them to check whether pairing worked.


LEAD 2: does a Duo do anything useful with storage?
---------------------------------------------------

The recorder and show-backup code paths are built for the Duo and the engine answers
both, byte-clean, but **nobody here has ever put a card or drive in a Duo**, so nothing
has been witnessed working.

Three things surface the moment somebody does: whether slot 1 is a usable storage port on
a Duo at all, whether it pushes `recordState` the way a Pro 2 does, and whether a Duo
show carries what a Pro 2 show carries.

Any Duo owner with a card can answer all three in one sitting.


LEAD 3: the USB dropout. Is it your unit too?
---------------------------------------------

The commands that make the unit reload its whole settings tree -
`showControlNewFromDefault` (factory reset) and `showControlImport` (load a show backup)
- leave it unresponsive roughly one time in five. Measured here: 2 failures in 9 resets
and 5 in 31 imports, against 0 failures in 9 full profile restores, which are far heavier
operations over the same interface.

The unit stays electrically present on the bus for about a minute answering nothing, then
Windows surprise-removes it. Replugging USB does not recover it; only pulling the mains
does, and it returns about 26 seconds after power is restored.

**What would help.** The same trials on a different unit, ideally on a different host.
Ten factory resets from the unit's own touchscreen with all third-party software closed
would be even better, because it takes every line of our code out of the picture. And if
it happens on Windows, the System event log entry: `Kernel-PnP` event 1010 naming
`VID_19F7`, followed by 1011 about 22 seconds later.

Expect roughly 1 in 5, so three clean runs prove nothing: ten leaves about an 11% chance
of seeing nothing by luck alone.


LEAD 4: can anything make the desk expose its recordings over USB?
------------------------------------------------------------------

The recordings drive does not mount over the desk's USB cable. `storageVolumeTransfer`
mounts nothing at any `transferModeType` from 0 to 8.

The firmware names the mechanism: storage is exposed by binding a FILE to a USB gadget
LUN (`.../mass_storage.usb0/lun.0/file`), and the engine only ever binds the pad image or
the update image - never the recordings partition. So the question is no longer "which
transfer mode" but "can anything we send make it bind a different file".

"You have to unplug the drive" is an acceptable answer. We just have not proved it.


LEAD 5: wireless mic telemetry has never seen a real transmitter
-----------------------------------------------------------------

`txConnected`, `txBatteryLevel`, `txRssi` and the rest of that family are read and
surfaced, but nothing has ever been paired here, so only the "two slots, both off" branch
has ever run and every reading carries an unproven flag.

Anyone with a RODE wireless transmitter can settle the read path, and can also confirm by
ear whether muting a transmitter from software actually mutes it. That write is built.


LEAD 6: is the Duo's Headset channel a microphone or a line input?
-------------------------------------------------------------------

We classify the Duo's source 2 (the 3.5mm TRRS headset jack) as a line input, and its
Level control writes `inputDigitalGain`, which reads 0.

But its node looks exactly like a microphone's: `inputType` 0, `inputMicrophoneGain` 50,
`inputPower`, `inputMicrophoneType`. RODE's own guide calls it "a dedicated headset input
channel ... to control the headset microphone's input level".

So the Level control may be writing the wrong PROPERTY at the right address. An engine
probe moved `inputPower` there but did NOT move the gain, where Mic 1 and Mic 2 both do.

**This needs an actual headset in the 3.5mm jack**, which nobody here has. Reading the
model again will not help: an empty mic input reads identically to this.


LEAD 7: properties nothing uses, and nobody knows what they do
---------------------------------------------------------------

Readable, present in a factory dump, and unclaimed: `appOutputDevice` (string, empty),
`appMonitorMix`, `appRecording`, and `eqParamModeHigh` / `Mid` / `Low`. The names suggest
they hold RODE's own app's preferences on the desk's behalf, which would make them inert
for anyone else. The three `eqParamMode` values sit beside `debugMode` and `metering`, so
a touchscreen presentation setting is more likely than anything audible.

Also unclaimed and more promising: `systemName`, `assignableMeterSource`, `presenterMode`
/ `presenterSoftware`, `shareAnom` / `shareResource`.

One write and a read-back settles each. Mind the sweep warning at the top.


LEAD 8: `padRecordStateRequest`
--------------------------------

The desk has a PADRECORDER block - recording straight to a sound pad. Our reading is that
it stores values and never acts on them, but that verdict was reached in a session where
a stray existed, and a stray accepts writes and does nothing. So it may never have been
tested at all.

It is a `pair` property, meaning it carries two operands, and we have not worked out the
layout. Do not guess one: a malformed write is how strays get made. Work it out from a
capture of RODE's own software first.


WHAT WE ALREADY SETTLED, SO YOU DO NOT REPEAT IT
-------------------------------------------------

`docs/PROJECT_GUIDE.md` part 2 has the full list with the mechanism for each. The short
version of the dead ends: `toneGeneration` makes no sound at any value; `broadcastMeters`
never carries anything; `emergencyMuteActive` does not mute a playing pad;
`staticIpSet` is a flag with no operands; `systemDateTimezone` is applied by the
touchscreen rather than the engine; the desk's own player is touchscreen-bound; and a
show does not carry Bluetooth pairing.
