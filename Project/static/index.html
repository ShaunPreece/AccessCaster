Keyboard shortcuts

These work anywhere in Windows, whatever program you are in and even with this window closed, for as long as AccessCaster is running. Each one needs Ctrl, Alt or Windows in it, so it cannot take a plain key away from your screen reader, and each normally speaks what it did, because when you press it you will be looking at something else.

Turning this off releases every combination, so they go back to whatever other programs do with them.

Puts the whole pad set on the number row in one go, on Ctrl, Alt and Shift like everything else: the number keys play the pads of the bank the unit is on, Dash and Equals step down and up a bank, and P says what is playing. Anything you have already set is left alone, and if another program has one of these keys the shortcut says so rather than failing quietly.

What a second press does is the pad's own setting, made on the SMART pads page or on the unit. A sound pad set to Toggle stops, one set to One Shot starts again from the beginning, and one set to Hold plays until you press the key again. A mixer pad follows its own trigger setting the same way.

Reset all output mixes

Puts every output back to its normal mix in one go: your headphones, the monitor speaker, the recording and USB 1 back to Main Mix, and Bluetooth, Chat, USB 2 and CallMe back to Mix Minus. It also puts every source back in each output and back to following the faders. Use this if a headphone output has been left on a Custom mix, which stops the Mute buttons silencing a channel in your own headphones. These are sensible everyday settings rather than the unit's exact factory state, and this wipes any custom mixes you have set, including "Chat = my mic only", so save a mix preset or a profile first if you want to keep them. You are asked to confirm. This button is the "Everything normal" mix preset above; the two do exactly the same thing.

Fader assignment

Choose which channel sits on each fader. Every fader can be reassigned, mic faders included; each one is labelled with whether it is a physical or a virtual fader. Putting a channel on a fader that already holds it elsewhere swaps the two, so you can rearrange your layout. A channel's processing and effects move with it on both units, so a voicing stays with its channel wherever you put it. Nothing is sent to the unit until you press that fader's own Apply button, so you can arrow through the choices safely. The current layout is read from the unit every time you open this tab.

Puts your unit's standard fader layout back (its default channel for each fader).

Stereo linked inputs

Link two inputs that are next to each other and they share one fader, panned hard left and right, so a stereo source (a keyboard, a pair of overheads, a stereo mic) rides on a single fader with both sides matched. Only neighbouring MIC inputs can be linked, so a Pro 2 offers 1 and 2, 2 and 3, or 3 and 4, while a Duo has the one pair, 1 and 2, because its third input is the headset jack. The switches below are built from your own unit, so what you see is what it has. Linking frees the second input's fader; when you unlink, that input goes back onto a free fader and its processing comes back with it. Each switch takes effect on the unit as soon as you toggle it.

Fader and monitor levels

The virtual fader and monitor speaker levels, which have no fader on the unit. Read when you open the tab, and sent as you move them.

Each virtual fader has its mute and solo here too: the unit's encoder reaches its level, but nothing on the unit reaches its mute or solo. Mute silences that channel everywhere, recordings and streams included. Solo changes your headphones only.

Output mutes and Bluetooth level

These silence a whole OUTPUT, which is not the same as muting a channel. Nothing here changes your mixes, your recording or anything you are streaming: only what somebody hears. The Bluetooth output is what a paired phone hears FROM the desk, which is the opposite direction to the Bluetooth channel on the Channels page.

Auto-mute

Auto-mute drops the monitor or Bluetooth output automatically when a microphone is live, so the unit's own speakers cannot feed back into the mics.

Master Mix

USB audio

What the unit sends this computer over its main USB connection, and what it accepts back: what any program here hears from the unit, whether it is recording, streaming or in a call, and whether programs here arrive on one fader or four. It does not change what the unit writes to its own card or drive: that is on the Recording page, Alt plus 4.

Master output processing

The Master Compellor is an output compressor that evens out and glues your whole mix; it applies to the Main Mix outputs only. Output delay holds all Main Mix outputs back by up to 2000 milliseconds to line audio up with video. These change your main output for everyone, so adjust with care.

SMART pads

Each choice reads out as bank, pad number, then what is on it, so you can arrow straight to the pad you want.

Settings for this pad

Each of these takes effect as soon as you change it, except the name, which needs the Rename button beside it. There is no Apply button on this tab. To change what the pad does, or to empty it, use the Reassign tab.

This pad is empty, so it has no settings yet. Put a sound, a voice effect, a mixer action or a MIDI trigger on it from the Reassign tab, then come back here to set its volume, colour and how it plays. The button below takes you to that tab.

Slide to set how loud the pad plays, from minus 60 up to plus 12 decibels. It applies a moment after you stop moving it.
In these dropdowns, arrow to a choice and press Enter to apply it.
Toggle means one press starts the sound and the next press stops it. Hold means it plays only while you hold the pad down. One Shot means each press replays it from the beginning and it cannot be stopped, so Loop is turned off in that mode.

Latching means one press turns the action on and the next press turns it off. Momentary means it is only on while you hold the pad down.
Trim and fade

Where in the sound the pad starts and stops, and where it fades up and down, each as a percentage of the whole sound. Leave a fade point on the same percentage as the start or the stop for no fade at that end. Each change takes effect straight away.

Fade times

How long this pad takes to fade the mix down and back up, in seconds. Each change takes effect straight away.

Censor sound

A Censor pad normally lays the built-in beep over everything. It can use a sound of your own instead, but that sound has to be put on the unit itself; this app does not upload one, so switching this on without one leaves the pad with nothing to play.

What this pad sends

A MIDI trigger pad sends one MIDI message to your computer when you press it, so software on the computer can react to a pad on the desk. Out of the box that is a Control Change on the pad's own number, counting from zero, on channel 1, with 127 when pressed and 0 when released. Switch on the custom mapping below to change any of that. Each change takes effect straight away.

Switching the custom mapping OFF puts the pad back on the default and does not keep the numbers you had set, so it is not undone by switching it on again. The unit itself works that way; it is not a limit of this app.

Who is in the Back Channel

Back Channel is a private talkback room. While the pad is on, everyone ticked here, plus Mic 1, is taken out of the main mix, so nothing they say is recorded, streamed or heard by anyone else, and instead they hear each other. Mic 1 is always included, so tick the people you want to talk to. Tick nothing and the pad does nothing. Each tick applies straight away.

    Pad maintenance

    Jobs that act on the whole grid rather than on the pad chosen above, and each asks you to confirm before it does anything. The two exports are the exception: they act on the pad chosen above and ask nothing, because neither changes the unit.

    Clear all pads

    Empties every soundpad, voice-effect, mixer-action and MIDI pad on the grid. The built-in System pads (the fader and scene controls) are left alone. This is reversible only by setting the pads again, so you are asked to confirm first.

    Restore the factory voice effects, Censor and Ducking

    Puts the six factory voice effects back on their own pads, and the Censor and Ducking pads with them, each with its factory name, colour and settings. Only those eight pad positions are touched: everything past pad 16 is left alone, and so are the factory sounds, which this cannot restore. Those live on the unit itself and come back when you restore a backup that has them, or when you start again from factory settings. It overwrites whatever is on those eight pads now, so you are asked to confirm first. It takes two or three minutes: each pad is cleared and rebuilt one at a time, and the unit needs a moment between each.

    Export a pad's sound to your computer

    Copies the sound from the pad chosen at the top of this page into your Documents folder, under AccessCaster, Pad exports, named after the pad. Nothing on the unit is changed. Only sound pads have a file to export: a voice effect is a recipe the unit builds, not a recording. Use this to get a sound off the unit if it is the only copy you have left, or to edit it and put it back.

    Tidy up unused pad audio

    Deletes sound files from the unit that no pad plays any more, which frees up its pad storage. Most of the time there is nothing to find: replacing a pad, restoring the pad defaults, clearing all pads and restoring a profile with pads all clear up after themselves. What this catches is clearing pads one at a time, which leaves each sound behind, and any leftovers already on the unit. It only ever removes files no pad points at, so nothing you can currently play is touched, and it cannot be undone.

    Backups

    A backup holds the unit's settings and your SMART pads: channels, processing, routing, faders, the unit's own settings, and every pad with its sound. Two things are left out on purpose: which channels are muted, since putting a mute back could silence someone at the worst moment, and passwords, so wifi and phone-call credentials are never stored.

    Start again from factory settings

    This puts every setting and every SMART pad back to how the unit left the factory. Your wifi, the language and the analytics answer are not part of it and are left alone, and so are the recordings on the drive. The unit backs itself up first, so this can be undone by restoring that backup. It does not use the unit's touchscreen setup wizard, so nothing is left needing a sighted person to tap through.

    This app's own settings

    It starts in the system tray without opening this page, so your shortcut keys work from the moment you sign in. Only for your own Windows account.

    Your keyboard shortcuts, announcement preferences, level warnings and CallMe contacts belong to AccessCaster rather than to the unit, so no profile carries them and reinstalling would lose them. Saving them here writes one small file you can copy to another computer or keep somewhere safe. It contains nothing about the desk and no remote-access token, so it is safe to email to yourself.

    Choose a file, then press Restore. Your shortcuts start working straight away, without restarting. Anything naming a voice or an audio device this computer has not got is left alone and said out loud, rather than being applied to nothing.
