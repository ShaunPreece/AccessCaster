About AccessCaster

AccessCaster lets you use your RODECaster (Pro 2 or Duo) audio desk without the RODE software, and to a large degree without ever touching the unit's touchscreen.

As a blind screen reader user, I found the official RODE software was not usable enough for me: I needed help every time I wanted to set the desk up or change a setting.

With AccessCaster I can control and adjust most of the desk's features, including processing, routing, recording, SMART pad management, and device settings such as wifi and Bluetooth connections, all of it accessible to a screen reader. I have also added things the desk does not give you at all: spoken announcements for every button on the unit, fader positions read out as you move them, and keyboard shortcuts you can put on over 100 of the app's commands, such as starting a recording or loading a mic processing preset.

The app was built with Claude Code, and took many months of capturing the USB traffic between the RODE app and my own RODECaster Pro 2 to work out how the desk talks. I have tried to keep the code as clean and efficient as I can, and I now use this app exclusively to run my desk. The interface is HTML rather than a native Windows one, so that moving it to a Mac later on is straightforward.

There are things AccessCaster cannot do, and they fall into two quite different groups. The first I am still working on. The second I am not, because the unit does not offer them over the USB cable at all, and no software on this computer can change that: they are here so you know where the edge is rather than waiting for something that is not coming.

Still to come:

  • Recording, and the desk's own backups (Shows), on a Duo. Both are switched on and both are answered by the unit, but no Duo I can reach has a card or a drive in it, so nobody has yet watched one make a recording or write a backup. Put storage in a Duo and it should simply work; until somebody has done it, I am not going to tell you it does. Recording matters more on a Duo than it sounds, because a Duo has no record button on it at all. Everything else a Duo used to be locked out of now works there: the unit settings, the virtual fader and monitor levels, the network settings, stereo linking, and a channel's processing following it to a new fader.
  • Listening back to a recording, or copying one off. Starting, stopping, marking and deleting all work, as do what gets recorded and whether it is saved as WAV or MP3. The recordings drive does not appear on this computer over the unit's cable, so today a take is fetched by taking the drive out and plugging it in here, where it is an ordinary removable disk.
  • Backing up your Bluetooth pairings, your wifi network and a wireless mic pairing. A backup can neither save nor restore any of them, so they are the settings you would have to put back by hand after a factory reset. Joining a wifi network and making the unit discoverable are both in the app, on the Device page under Wireless connections; the Bluetooth pairing itself is made from the phone.

Not coming, because the unit does not expose them:

  • Searching for Bluetooth devices or starting a pairing from this end. Making the unit discoverable, and pairing from your phone, does work. A RODECaster Pro 2 ignores a search request when it arrives over USB; this was tested three separate ways rather than assumed.
  • Setting the unit's date and time, its time zone, or daylight saving. It sets its own clock over the internet, and only the touchscreen applies a time zone or daylight saving change: the unit stores one sent from here and leaves the clock where it was.
  • Setting a fixed IP address. This is the unit's own limitation rather than mine, and it was tested on the desk rather than assumed: it reports the address, subnet and gateway it currently has, but gives no way to set them.
  • Recording straight onto a pad. The unit reboots when asked to, every time, so this one is deliberately not offered.
  • Installing a firmware update. The app tells you when one is available; installing it still needs the unit's screen or RODE's own app.
  • Reading the unit's own level meters. The level readings come from the audio it sends this computer over USB instead, which is close but is not the same meter.

AccessCaster is not endorsed by, associated with, or supported by RODE. It is an independent project, and RODE have had no hand in it. I have taken every precaution I can to make sure the code is safe and that nothing it does to the desk cannot be undone, but it talks directly to your hardware, so you use it at your own risk.

Have fun, and I hope it helps.
Shaun.

What AccessCaster can control

The features that are working today. Everything here goes over the same USB connection the RODE software uses, and every value you see has been read back off the unit, so what the app shows is the desk's live state rather than the last command somebody sent it. Changes made on the unit itself show up here too.

  • Mute on every channel, the same mute as the button on the unit, so you can silence a channel from a phone or another room. Muting on the unit shows here too.
  • Listen (solo) on every channel, the same solo as the button on the unit: it sends just that channel to your headphones and leaves your mixes, the recording and anything you are streaming alone. A Clear every solo button sits beside it, so you never have to work out which channel left the desk sounding dead.
  • Muting a RODE wireless microphone at the transmitter, from the mic's own channel: not the same as muting the channel, which would leave an open mic in the room still feeding the desk.
  • Per-channel settings: mic gain, 48V phantom power, polarity and mic type on each mic, plus the line level on the other channels (such as Bluetooth, USB, Headset and Chat).
  • Setting a mic's gain by ear rather than by eye: talk for half a minute and the app measures the channel and sets the gain for you, with an undo if you do not like it. There is also a level check on every channel that speaks the level as you go and then the loudest it got, with the fader taken out of the reading.
  • Full on-board processing on every input channel: equaliser, compressor, noise gate, de-esser, high-pass filter, tone shaping and pan, with ready-made mic presets and your own saved presets. The SMART pads mix is the exception, and that is the unit's own limit rather than ours: it has a mute, a solo and a fader, and the unit applies no processing to it.
  • Per-channel voice effects (reverb and echo) and their settings.
  • Master output processing: the Master Compellor and an output delay for lining audio up with video.
  • Fader assignment for every fader on your unit, with a one-press reset to the default layout.
  • Output routing: each output's mix (Main Mix, Mix Minus, or a custom per-source mix), read live from the unit, plus mix presets, which save the whole set of mixes under a name so you can put them all back in one press.
  • Monitor mix levels: set how loud each source is in any one output, separately from the recording.
  • The virtual fader levels and the monitor speaker level, which have no physical fader to grab.
  • A one-press reset of every output mix back to its normal state.
  • What the unit sends this computer over USB (one mixed pair or every channel separately), monitor and Bluetooth auto-mute, and headphone type.
  • What this computer sends the unit: either as one stereo input, or split into four separate ones (Game, Music, A and B), each on its own fader, so music, a call, a game and your screen reader arrive as four channels instead of one and can be faded apart. Set it up with RODE's Virtual Devices driver installed and the app points Windows' sound output at the unit for you.
  • Muting a whole output: the monitor speakers, or the Bluetooth send, plus the level a paired phone hears from the desk. None of these touch your mixes, your recording or your streams, only what somebody hears.
  • The unit's own recorder: start, pause, resume and stop, drop a marker while you record, hear what it is doing and how long it has been going, read the list of what is already on the card or drive, and delete a recording you no longer want. What it records, a single mixed track or every channel on its own, is set here too, as is whether it saves a full-quality WAV or a smaller MP3.
  • CallMe guests: switch on hosting, read out the unit's code and copy the link a guest opens in their browser, hear who is calling, answer or decline from the page or from a keyboard shortcut in any program, call another RODECaster by its code or from your contacts, hang up, and hear when a call connects and ends. Extend a call before the plan's one-hour limit, or have it extended for you. The call's length, its quality and the minutes left on your plan are on the page too, and the CallMe channel has its own level and processing on the Channels page. The touchscreen is otherwise the only way to take a call.
  • How the unit's clock is shown: 24-hour or 12-hour, and whether the time appears on the unit's screen. Also three lockouts that can lock the unit's physical buttons or silence every headphone or line output.
  • Level meters in words: a background watch that stays quiet until it has news, telling you when a stream clips or goes silent, and an optional level spoken on a timer while you set gains. Read from the unit's USB audio streams through Windows rather than from the unit's own meters.
  • SMART pads: put sounds, voice effects, mixer actions or MIDI triggers on any pad; rename, and set volume, colour, loop and restart behaviour; trim a sound and fade it in and out; clear one pad or all of them; and build your own custom voice effect with a live preview. Mixer pads add latching or momentary triggering, and Back Channel pads let you pick who is in the off-air room. You can also copy a pad's sound back to this computer, and tidy up sound files no pad plays any more.
  • Backups: one list and one button. The unit keeps its own complete copy on its card or drive when there is one, this computer keeps the settings and pads either way, and a restore uses whichever is still there.
  • The unit's own backups: back the whole desk up to the recordings drive in seconds, delete the ones you no longer want, and start again from factory settings without needing the unit's touchscreen wizard. Restoring one of these the unit's own way is the fallback for a backup this computer has no copy of, and the dialog tells you when that is the case.
  • A Current state summary of the whole desk in one place: what is muted or soloed, every fader level, anything unusual about the routing, and the pad bank.
  • Hardware status the touchscreen would otherwise keep to itself: whether a memory card is in and how much room is left, the recorder, the firmware, and whether a network cable is in.
  • Wireless connections in one place: turning the unit's wifi on, scanning for networks and joining one, making the unit discoverable so a phone can pair with it, and what wireless mics the unit can see.
  • The settings the unit otherwise offers only on its touchscreen: screen brightness and whether it sets itself, how long before the screen dims, how brightly the buttons are lit, the meter style, haptic feedback, whether the record button pauses or stops, the listen mode, and the monitor output's fixed level. A backup holds them.
  • Spoken announcements of what the unit is doing: every physical button press, the banks, the encoder, and fader and level moves read out as you make them, in a voice, at a volume and through an audio output of your choosing.
  • Keyboard shortcuts that work anywhere in Windows, whatever program you are in, built from a catalogue of over 100 things the app can do. You start with seven of your own plus the SMART pad keys, and add or remove whichever you want.
  • Playing SMART pads from the keyboard: the number row plays the pads of the bank the unit is on, two more keys step through the banks, and one more says what is playing and how long is left. Sound pads, voice effects and mixer pads alike, with no need to reach the unit.
  • Control from a phone or another computer on the same network, over an access link that keeps everyone else on that network out. The computer the unit is plugged into never needs the link, and you can make a new one at any time to lock out a device you have shared it with.
  • Device info: connection, firmware and network status, and the MIDI control unlock that lets an external MIDI app or controller drive the desk.

Help: what each page is for

A plain-language guide to every page and what is on it. Use the links at the top, or Alt and the number shown. Press H in your screen reader to jump between the pages below.

When the app opens, you start on its own heading at the very top, which names the app and the version you are running. Underneath it, the unit you are connected to is read out as soon as it has been found, and again if it is ever unplugged. The page links are one Tab below, and the page itself is one press of H away. Moving to another page puts you on that page's heading. One more control sits after the last page link, on every page: a button that turns dark mode off and on, whose label tells you which way it is set.

One thing worth knowing about the Tab key: the end of a page is a hard stop. Tab on the last control does not wrap round to the top, it stays where it is and plays a short low tone, so you can tell you have reached the end rather than quietly finding yourself back at the beginning. Shift and Tab still walks backwards, and headings, landmarks, the links at the top and Alt with a number all still get you anywhere on the page.

And one about dropdowns. In any dropdown that changes the unit, arrowing through the list only browses, and Enter applies the choice you are on. Moving away without pressing Enter puts it back to what the unit has, and the app says so. That is because arrowing through a closed dropdown is how a screen reader reads one: otherwise, browsing the mic type list would put your mic through four different preamps on the way past. Each of these dropdowns says "Press Enter to apply" when you reach it. With a mouse, picking an option applies it straight away. Dropdowns that only change what you are LOOKING at, such as which channel or which output you are working on, act straight away, because there is nothing to undo. Switches are instant too: you cannot change one by arrowing past it.

Where everything is on a RODECaster Pro 2

The desk as it sits in front of you, described from where you are sitting. The Duo is below.

The landmark
The touchscreen. It is in the top left and it stands proud of the surface like a wedge, so it is the easiest thing on the desk to find by feel. Everything else is described from it.
Top left
The RODE logo in the corner, and just below it, on its own, the round REC button.
Top right
Four small knobs in a row: the headphone volumes, 1 on the left through to 4 on the right. Below them, at the left of that group, the one large knob, which is the encoder.
The faders
Six of them across the lower half, left to right, faders 1 to 6. Each has a square button above it, that channel's button: pressing it hands the encoder to that channel, so the big knob then sets that channel's gain or level, and pressing it again hands the encoder back. Below each fader are two small buttons side by side: listen on the left, mute on the right.
The SMART pads
Eight pads down the right-hand side, in two columns of four. They are numbered down the columns, not across the rows: the top left pad is 1, with 2, 3 and 4 below it, and the top right pad is 5, with 6, 7 and 8 below that. Directly underneath them, two buttons: previous bank on the left, next bank on the right.
The back panel
Left to right as you sit in front of the desk, so the far left end is microphone input 1 and the far right end is the power. In order: the four combo XLR inputs, 1 to 4; the two monitor outputs, which are stacked one above the other, left on top and right below; the four headphone jacks, a block of two rows of two, 1 and 2 along the top and 3 and 4 directly beneath them; then USB 2 and USB 1; then the ethernet socket, with the microSD card slot in the same recess directly above it; then a security lock slot; and last the power inlet, a USB-C mounted vertically rather than flat, with the round power button at the very end. Worth knowing, because this app talks to the unit over one cable and not the other: USB 1 is the nearer of the two to the power end.
What is not on the desk
Faders 7, 8 and 9 have no physical fader: they are USB 1, USB 1 Chat and USB 2, and they exist on the touchscreen and in this app only.

Where everything is on a RODECaster Duo

The same desk in a smaller frame: half the inputs, two thirds of the pads, and the same arrangement in the same places.

The landmark
The touchscreen, which fills the left half of the top surface. Everything else is described from it.
There is no REC button
The Duo has no record button anywhere on it, so on this desk the touchscreen, or this app, is the only way to start a recording.
Top right
Two small knobs side by side: the headphone volumes, 1 on the left and 2 on the right. Below them, the one large knob, which is the encoder.
The faders
Four of them, left to right, faders 1 to 4, under the screen. Above the row of faders is a row of four square buttons, one per fader: pressing one hands the encoder to that channel, so the big knob then sets that channel's gain or level, and pressing it again hands the encoder back. Below each fader are two small buttons side by side: listen on the left, mute on the right.
The SMART pads
Six pads down the right-hand side, in two columns of three. They are numbered down the columns, not across the rows: the top left pad is 1, with 2 and 3 below it, and the top right pad is 4, with 5 and 6 below that. Directly underneath them, two buttons: previous bank on the left, next bank on the right.
The front edge
One 3.5mm socket, which is the headset input: a headset's microphone and its earpieces on the one plug. It hears the same mix as headphone output 1.
The back panel
Left to right as you sit in front of the desk, so the far left end is microphone input 1 and the far right end is the power. In order: the two combo XLR inputs, 1 and 2; the two monitor outputs, stacked one above the other, left on top and right below; the two headphone jacks, also stacked, 1 on top and 2 below; then USB 2 and USB 1; then the ethernet socket, with the microSD card slot in the same recess directly above it; then a security lock slot; and last the power inlet, a USB-C mounted vertically, with the red power button at the very end.
What is not on the desk
The Duo has nine faders and only four you can put a finger on, so faders 5 to 9 live on the touchscreen and in this app only. By default the four physical ones carry Mic 1, Mic 2, Bluetooth and the SMART pads; of the five you cannot reach, four carry the headset input, USB 1, Chat and USB 2, and the ninth ships empty for you to put something on.

Channels and Processing (Alt+1)

Pick one channel and set everything for it in one place. This covers every input channel your unit has: its microphones and its line channels (such as Bluetooth, USB, Headset, Chat and CallMe 1, the remote caller), plus the SMART pads mix, which has a mute and a solo here but no level and no processing: its level is its own fader, and the unit applies no processing to the pads. The list of channels matches the device you are connected to.

Channel
Choose which channel you are working on. The controls below change to match it. This one acts straight away, with no Enter needed: it changes what you are looking at, not what the desk is doing.
Tabs
Everything under the channel is on a tab strip: Channel settings, Presets, Processing, Equaliser, Dynamics, Tone and pan, and Effects on a mic. Any other channel has five of those, without Presets or Effects. The SMART pads mix has only Channel settings, because the unit applies no processing to it at all. The strip is one tab stop; move along it with the left and right arrow keys, Home and End for the ends, and the panel below follows as you go. Only the tab you are on is on the page, so nothing else is in your tab order or in heading navigation. Where a tab holds more than one thing (Dynamics holds the compressor, the noise gate and the de-esser) each keeps its own heading, so H walks you to it. The tab you leave off on is remembered, on this channel, the next channel and the next time you start the app.
Channel settings
Mute comes first on every channel: it is the same mute as the button on the unit, so it silences that channel in every mix, including the recording, and pressing the button on the unit changes it here too. Listen (solo) is next to it, with a Clear every solo button beside that. On a mic there is then a third switch, Mute the wireless mic, which is a different thing again and is explained below. Then for mics: gain (how hard the unit drives the signal, too low sounds quiet and too high distorts), 48V phantom power, polarity, and mic type. The line channels, Bluetooth, USB, Headset and Chat, have a level instead.
Mute and Listen, and the difference
Mute silences that channel for everybody: your headphones, the other outputs, the recording and anything you are streaming. Listen (solo) is the opposite and it is headphone-only: it sends just that channel to your headphones and leaves every mix, the recording and your streams exactly as they were. That makes it the safe way to check a mic mid-show, but the desk gives no clue you have left it on, so while anything is soloed you will not hear the app's announcements either. Clear every solo is the way back, and it is right there so you never have to work out which channel did it.
Mute the wireless mic, and why it is not the same as Mute
This one is for a RODE wireless microphone paired to the channel, and it mutes the TRANSMITTER rather than the channel. The difference matters when somebody walks off still wearing one: muting the channel leaves an open microphone in the room feeding the desk, while muting the transmitter means nothing arrives at all. It only appears on mic channels, because nothing can be paired to a USB, Bluetooth or Chat channel. If no wireless mic is paired there is nothing for it to reach, so the app tells you the mute was sent rather than claiming the mic went quiet.
48V phantom power, in one line
Condenser microphones need power sent up the mic cable to work at all, and that is what 48V is. A dynamic mic (a PodMic, an SM7B, most broadcast mics) does not need it and is not harmed by it. If a condenser is plugged in and silent, this switch is the first thing to check.
Set gain automatically
Mics only, and it is the accessible version of the one studio job that is normally done by eye: talk, watch a meter, turn the knob. Press it, talk normally for half a minute, and the app measures what arrived and sets the gain to match, telling you what it changed and offering an undo. Speak at the volume you will actually use, not louder.
Check this channel's level
On every channel except Chat. It speaks the level as it goes, for half a minute unless you have changed the length on the Level warnings tab, and then tells you the loudest it got. The fader position is taken out of the reading, so it answers "is this channel arriving at a sensible level" rather than telling you where you have the fader set. The fader does have to be up far enough for the channel to reach the Chat mix, which is what the check listens on; with it right down there is nothing to hear, and the check says so and tells you to bring it up. Use it on a phone on Bluetooth or a computer on USB as well as on mics, since those arrive at a level nobody chose either.
Mic type
Which microphone is on that input. The unit uses it to set the preamp up, and choosing one also loads that mic's own default gain, so expect the Level slider to move when you apply it. Nothing happens until you press Enter.
Processing presets
Mics only. Load a ready-made voicing like Podcast Studio, or save the current settings as your own preset. Like every dropdown that changes the unit, arrowing through the list changes nothing and Enter applies. The list shows the preset the channel is on, or says it has been adjusted since. Delete a preset opens its own list of your presets, since a preset goes from every channel and cannot be brought back.
Processing and modules
The studio chain. Switch it on as a whole or module by module: equaliser (tone), compressor (evens out loud and quiet parts), noise gate (cuts hiss between words), de-esser (tames harsh "s" sounds), high-pass filter (removes low rumble), tone shaping and pan. Each of those has its own group of sliders below this one. There is also an Advanced processing switch here: with it off the unit works the modules out from Depth, Sparkle and Punch, so their values move on their own, and its own hint on the page explains what changes.
Echo and Reverb
Mics only. The two channel effects, each with its own switch and settings.

Routing (Alt+2)

Where every sound goes: what each output can hear, and at what level.

Tabs
This page is a tab strip of four: Output mixes (which also holds mix presets and Reset all output mixes), Faders, Levels (the fader and monitor levels, the output mutes, auto-mute, the monitor mix levels and the headphone types), and Master Mix (the USB audio going each way between the unit and this computer, then the Compellor and the output delay). Arrow left and right along the strip, Home and End for the ends, and only the tab you are on is on the page. Opening a tab reads that tab's settings from the unit, so the values are always current, and the tab you leave off on is remembered.
Output mixes
The heart of routing. Every output (your headphones, USB to a computer, the Chat channel to callers, the Bluetooth send to a phone, and so on) carries its own mix, and they are separate from one another: changing what a caller hears does not change what you hear. Main Mix is everyone. Mix Minus is everyone except that output's own source. Custom lets you include or exclude each source for that one output by hand. Pick an output at the top and the list below shows the real mix, read live from the unit. The output you pick acts straight away, since it only changes what you are looking at; the mix you choose for it waits for Enter.
What Mix Minus actually means
It is the answer to feedback and echo on a call. Say a guest is on the phone over Bluetooth. Whatever the guest says comes INTO the desk on the Bluetooth channel, and if the desk sends its full mix back out to them, their own voice goes with it and they hear themselves a fraction of a second late, which is horrible to talk over. Mix Minus sends them the mix MINUS themselves: they hear you, your co-host and the pads, but not their own voice. The same applies to a caller on Chat or on USB. That is why the caller outputs offer Mix Minus and your headphones do not: you are not a source arriving on your own headphone output, so there is nothing to leave out.
Building a mix minus by hand
On a Custom mix, an output's own source is in the list like any other, worded "back into itself". Untick it and you have a mix minus you control: everything else about that mix stays yours. This is what you want with a second computer on USB 2. You hear it in your headphones, because that is a different output, but its own sound does not go back down the USB it arrived on, so nothing over there hears itself repeated. Mix Minus mode does the same leaving-out for you but decides the rest of the mix itself, which is why the hand-built version exists.
Mix presets
Output mixes saved under a name: which sources each output carries and at what level, put back in one press. It is not a backup of the desk and never touches your channels, processing or pads.
Which outputs a preset touches, and why it is in its name
Every preset in the list ends with its scope, because that is the thing you need to know before you choose one. "(every output)" rewrites the whole desk, so anything you have set by hand on another output goes back to what the preset holds. "(Chat only)", or any other output's name, touches that one mix and leaves the rest exactly as they are, which makes it the kind you can safely use in the middle of a show. The scope is worked out from the preset itself each time the list is read, so it is right even for a preset saved on the other RODECaster.
The three built-in presets
"Everything normal" puts every output back, the same thing as Reset all output mixes below, and it asks you to confirm because it wipes mixes you set by hand. "Chat = my mic only" sends a co-host on the Chat output just Mic 1 and nothing else, which is what you want when your screen reader is going into the mix and they should not hear it. "Chat back to normal" undoes that one, putting Chat back on its proper mix minus. The last two touch Chat alone.
Saving your own
Type a name, then choose which of the two save buttons you want. "Save every output's mix as a preset" stores the whole desk. "Save only the [output] mix as a preset" stores just the output you have chosen at the top of this tab, and its label always names that output, so it says what it will do without your having to check anything else. The one-output kind is worth knowing about: it is the only sort that cannot disturb a mix you are listening to, and it is the only sort worth putting on a keyboard shortcut.
Reset all output mixes
Puts every output back to its normal mix in one go, and every source back in and following the faders. Use it if a headphone output has been left on a Custom mix, which stops the Mute buttons silencing a channel in your own headphones. It wipes any custom mixes you have set, so save a mix preset or back the unit up first. You are asked to confirm.
Fader assignment
Choose which channel sits on each fader (your unit's physical faders plus its virtual ones), each one labelled with which kind it is. Every fader can be reassigned, mic faders included. Putting a channel where another already sits swaps them, so you can rearrange your layout. Nothing is sent until you press Enter on that fader. A reset puts the standard layout back. A channel's processing moves with it, on both units.
Fader and monitor levels
The levels with no physical fader to grab: the virtual faders and the monitor speaker level. Both units. If the encoder seems to have stopped working, a channel button has taken it: the announcer says which channel, and pressing that same button again gives the encoder back to these levels. Each virtual fader carries its mute and solo here as well, because those two have no control on the unit at all. If the desk ever sounds dead for no reason, a solo left on is the first thing to suspect, and "Clear all solos" on that tab is the one-press way out.
Output mutes and the Bluetooth level
These silence a whole OUTPUT, which is not the same as muting a channel: your mixes, the recording and anything you are streaming carry on untouched, and only what somebody hears goes quiet. There is one for the monitor speakers and one for the Bluetooth send, plus the level a paired phone hears from the desk. Worth keeping straight: the Bluetooth OUTPUT is what the phone hears from you, and the Bluetooth CHANNEL, on the Channels page, is what you hear from the phone.
Auto-mute
Two anti-feedback safety switches, on the Levels tab under the two manual mutes they automate. Monitor auto-mute silences your speakers whenever a mic fader is raised, so the mic does not pick the speakers up and squeal. Bluetooth auto-mute does the same for the Bluetooth output when a mic goes live. Leave both on if you use speakers or take calls.
Master Mix
Two things, both about the whole mix on its way out. USB audio comes first, because it is the one you come back to: it is the two directions of the cable to this computer. "What this computer receives" is what any program here hears from the desk, whether it is recording, streaming or in a call: either the mixed pair, or every channel separately so a program can record them apart. "What this computer sends" is the other way round, and is where Expanded lives, below. Then master output processing, which affects everyone, so adjust with care: the Master Compellor is an output compressor that evens out and glues the mix together, and output delay holds the main outputs back by up to two seconds to line audio up with video. Neither half changes what the unit writes to its own card or drive, which is on the Recording page.
Expanded: four more inputs from this computer
Normally this computer looks to the unit like one stereo input, so everything Windows plays arrives on a single channel already mixed together. Expanded splits it into four separate inputs instead, named Game, Music, A and B, each of which can sit on its own fader. Music, a call, a game and your screen reader then arrive as four channels rather than one, and you can fade or mute any of them without touching the rest.
What you would use it for
Three everyday ones. Play music in one program and have a guest on a call in another, and you can duck the music under their voice with a fader instead of going hunting for a volume slider. Put your screen reader on an input of its own, so you can leave it out of what a caller hears, or out of the recording, while still hearing it yourself. Or record a show with the music, the call and your microphone as separate tracks, so the balance is something you can fix afterwards rather than something you have to get right on the night.
What it needs
RODE's Virtual Devices driver, installed on this computer. Without it the four inputs exist on the unit but nothing here can send sound to them, and the page says which of those two you are looking at. One side effect worth knowing about: while it is installed the RODECaster ASIO driver stops working, which matters only if you use a program that asks for ASIO.
Getting the driver, and the one time this app uses the internet
If the driver is missing, a button appears beside that line to fetch it. It downloads RODE's own file, checks it really is signed by RODE, and then runs RODE's installer, which asks Windows for permission in the usual way and may want to restart the computer. It never installs anything behind your back. This is the only thing in AccessCaster that connects to the internet, and it only happens when you press that button: never when the app starts, never on a timer, and never to check whether a newer version exists. Everything else the app does stays between this computer and the USB cable. There is a second button beside it that just opens RODE's software page in your browser, so you can always do it by hand instead. That one stays on the page even once the driver is in, because it is also how you repair or reinstall it.
Sending a program to one of the four
The app points Windows' sound output at "System (RODECaster)" for you, and that is what carries your screen reader and anything you have not sent somewhere else. To put one particular program on one of the four, open Settings, System, Sound, then "App volume and device preferences" with that program running, and choose Game, Music, A or B as its output. Each one you use turns up on the unit as its own channel. If the one you want is not in that list, it is almost certainly not on a fader yet: see below.
An input only exists in Windows while it is on a fader
This is the one to remember. Game, Music, A and B appear in Windows' list of sound devices only while that channel is sitting on a fader, so turning Expanded on is half the job: until you assign them on the Faders tab, Windows has nothing to send sound to and the four look as though they have not arrived. Assign one and it turns up in Windows within a second or two; take it off its fader and it disappears again, along with whatever you had pointed at it. It is the unit's own rule rather than this app's, and it is the usual answer to "the driver is installed, so where are my devices".
Turning Expanded off again
Going back to Standard unassigns whatever you had put on Game, Music, A and B, and turning Expanded on again does not bring that layout back, so arrange your faders again afterwards. Either change also disconnects and reconnects the unit's audio devices for a couple of seconds, so anything playing through the unit, your screen reader included, goes quiet while it happens. Leaving Expanded takes "System (RODECaster)" away, so the app moves Windows' sound to your computer's own speakers for those seconds and back onto the unit's Main output once that is there, and says so.
Why your sound moves for a moment when Expanded comes on
RODE's driver can only connect to the unit while nothing in Windows is playing through the unit's own Main output. So while the unit reconnects in Expanded, the app moves Windows' sound to another output, such as your computer's own speakers, for a few seconds, and moves it on to "System (RODECaster)" once the driver has connected. If you hear your screen reader from the computer's speakers briefly, that is why. On a computer with no other output there is nowhere to move it, and the app reconnects the unit again if the driver misses it.
Nine separate inputs at once
Expanded and "Every channel, post-fader" together give this computer nine separate inputs to record or stream from: your microphones, the pads, the call channels and the four from this computer, each its own device in Windows and pickable in anything that lets you choose a microphone. That is the arrangement to ask for when you want everything recorded apart and mixed later.
Monitor mix levels
Set how loud each source is in one chosen output, without changing the others or the recording. For example, make your own mic quieter in your headphones while everyone else stays the same. Reset puts that output back to following the faders.
Headphones
The headphone type for Headphone 1 and Headphone 2, applied with the button beside each one. On a Pro 2, headphone outputs 3 and 4 are not reachable yet. High sensitivity is intended for quieter headphones and can be loud, so change with care.

SMART pads (Alt+3)

The SMART pads across the banks: what each pad does, plus a custom effect builder. This page sets pads up rather than playing them. To PRESS a pad, use a keyboard shortcut: the number row plays the pads of the bank the unit is on, and every kind of pad works, sound pads included. See Keyboard shortcuts on the Device page.

Tabs
The pad you are working on is chosen above the strip, because the first two tabs act on it. They are split by how they behave: nothing on Pad Type happens until you press Apply, and everything on Pad Settings takes effect the moment you change it. Then Voice effects, which is the effect builder and changes no pad at all: it saves an effect to the list you then pick from on Pad Type. Last is Maintenance, which is jobs on the whole grid, with one exception, the pad export, which acts on the pad you chose above. Arrow left and right along the strip, Home and End for the ends, and only the tab you are on is on the page. Opening Pad Type or Pad Settings reads the pads from the unit again, and the tab you leave off on is remembered.
Pad Type
What the pad is: a sound of your own, a voice effect, a mixer action or a MIDI trigger, the same types the unit's own screen offers, plus the one thing that defines it, such as the sound file. Everything you adjust afterwards, Back Channel's people included, is on Pad Settings. Nothing here touches the pad until you press Apply, the last control before Clear this pad, so until then the pad carries on as it is and Pad Settings still describes it. A successful Apply moves you to Pad Settings and says what is there for the new pad.
Pad Settings
The chosen pad as it is now: its name (with a Rename button, the one thing here that needs pressing), volume, colour, how it plays, whether a press restarts it, and looping. A mixer pad also gets its trigger mode, and a Back Channel pad who is in the room. There is no Apply: switches and sliders write as you change them, and the dropdowns apply on Enter. An empty pad has no settings, so it says so and offers a button through to Pad Type.
Trim and fade (sound pads)
Four points as a percentage of the whole sound: where playback starts, where it fades up to, where it fades down from, and where it stops. So a bed that should come in gently over its first quarter is Start 0, Fade in until 25. Leave a fade point on the same percentage as the start or the stop for no fade at that end, and if a fade point ends up outside the part being played the page tells you, because the unit accepts it and simply does nothing. Percentages rather than seconds because the unit does not report how long a sound is.
Fade times and censor sound (mixer pads)
A Fade pad carries how many seconds it takes to fade the mix down and back up, and whether your own mic is left out of the fade so you can still be heard. A Censor pad can use a sound of your own instead of the built-in beep, though that sound has to be put on the unit itself; the page says whether one is there.
What a MIDI trigger pad sends
On a MIDI trigger pad, Settings also carries the mapping: the message type (Control Change or Note), the control or note number, the MIDI channel, the value sent when you press and when you release, and whether it latches (press on, press again off) or is momentary (on only while held). Out of the box a pad sends a Control Change on its own number counting from zero, so bank 1 pad 1 is 0, on channel 1, with 127 pressed and 0 released. Those numbers only take effect with the custom mapping switched on, and switching it off puts the pad back on the default WITHOUT keeping what you typed, which is how the unit itself behaves.
Mixer actions explained
Censor lays a beep over everything; Trash Talk cuts your audio to callers while you still hear them; Fade In and Fade Out ramp the mix up or down; Back Channel is off-air talkback; Ducking lowers everyone but the host.
Create a custom voice effect
Build your own voice effect from reverb, echo and more, give it a name and save it; it then joins the voice-effect list ready to put on any pad. Live preview lets you hear it on your own mic as you build, and only touches the mic while it is switched on.
Your saved effects
The effects you have made, with a delete for each. Deleting one takes it off the list you choose from; it does not change any pad it is already on.
Pad maintenance
Jobs that act on the whole grid rather than on the pad chosen at the top, and every one that changes the unit asks you to confirm first. Clear all pads empties every sound, voice-effect, mixer and MIDI pad in one go, leaving the built-in System pads alone. Anything that copies sounds to the unit takes minutes rather than seconds, because each one goes over one pad at a time.
Restore the factory voice effects, Censor and Ducking
For when you have overwritten one of the six factory voice effects, or the Censor or Ducking pad, and want it back. Only those eight pad positions are touched; everything past pad 16 is left alone.

It cannot bring the factory SOUNDS back, and nothing in AccessCaster can. Those eight WAV files belong to RODE and live on the unit itself rather than in this app. Two things do bring them back: restoring a backup taken while they were still on the unit, which changes nothing else you have not asked for; or Start again from factory settings on the Backups page, which puts the whole unit back to how it left the factory. Try a backup first, because the factory reset occasionally leaves the unit needing its power pulled before it comes back.

Export a pad's sound, and open the exports folder
The exception on the Maintenance tab: these two act on the pad you chose at the top rather than on the whole grid, and neither asks you to confirm, because neither changes the unit. The export copies that pad's sound into your Documents folder, under AccessCaster, Pad exports, named after the pad. Use it when the unit holds the only copy of a sound, or to edit one and put it back. Only sound pads have a file to export: a voice effect is a recipe the unit builds, not a recording.
Tidy up unused pad audio
Deletes sound files from the unit that no pad plays any more. Replacing a pad's sound used to leave the old file behind, and it adds up. The app now clears up after itself when you replace a pad, restore the defaults, clear all pads or restore a backup with pads, so this is for what older versions left behind, or after clearing pads one at a time. It only ever removes files no pad points at, so nothing you can currently play is touched.

Recording (Alt+4)

Start, pause and stop the unit's own recorder, see where it will record to, and read what is already on the card or drive. It is a plain page rather than a tab strip, so Stop is always two keystrokes away. Recording started or stopped on the unit itself shows up here too, and is spoken if announcements are on.

What to record
First on the page, because it is the one decision here you cannot fix afterwards: press record on the wrong setting and the tracks you wanted do not exist. "Mixed track only" gives you one stereo file. The two separate-track choices give you that same mixed file AS WELL AS one file per channel, not instead of it, so nothing is lost by choosing them except space: about seven times as much, measured. On a Pro 2 that is ten files, the mix plus Combo 1 to 4, Bluetooth, Sounds, USB 1 Main, USB 1 Chat and USB 2. Pre-fader ignores where your faders are, so a guest who was too quiet on the night can be brought up later; post-fader keeps your fader moves, so the tracks sound as the show did. This is what the unit writes to its own card or drive; what it sends this computer over USB is a separate setting, on Routing under Master Mix.
Save recordings as
Underneath it, and the other thing you cannot change after the take: whether the unit writes a WAV or an MP3. WAV is uncompressed 24-bit, 48 kHz, and about eighteen times the size, which is what you want if the recording is going to be edited. MP3 is 128 kbps, which is fine for speech and fits far more on the card. There are only these two; the unit offers no in-between quality. This is the mixed stereo file only. If you are recording separate tracks, those are always 24-bit WAV whatever you choose here, so choosing MP3 gives you lossless tracks to work on plus a small mix to listen back to. A backup remembers your choice.
Record the separate tracks raw
Last of the three, and unlike the two dropdowns it takes effect the moment you switch it, with no Enter: a switch cannot be changed by arrowing past it, so there is nothing to protect you from. It decides what is IN the separate tracks, not how many you get or what format they are. Off, each track carries that channel's own processing, the EQ, compressor and gate. On, the tracks are the bare microphone or input, so you can do that work yourself afterwards. The mixed file always keeps the processing whichever way this is set, so the usual reason to turn it on is that you intend to edit properly later and would rather not have the unit's decisions baked in. It is only available once you have chosen and applied one of the separate-track modes, since it does nothing at all while only the mixed track is being made, and it says so while it is unavailable.
The record button
Its label follows the unit: Start recording, then Pause, then Resume recording. Every press reads the unit back before reporting, so what it says has happened really has.
Announcements while recording
With "Switch announcements off while recording" on, which is the default, spoken announcements stop when recording starts and come back when it stops, because anything played out loud is picked up by the microphones whichever output it goes to. Turn it off if your announcements go to headphones the microphones cannot hear, and you would rather keep hearing the unit during a take. If you had announcements switched off already, nothing changes either way. Your screen reader is separate and keeps talking, so if it is playing through the unit it goes into the recording: the usual answer is a second pair of headphones or earbuds on your computer's own output, with the announcement output on the Device page set to match.
Drop a marker
Puts a marker at the point you are at, so you can find that moment again when you edit. It goes into the recording as a standard marker, which editors show as a label or cue. Only available while recording, since a marker needs a position.
Recording status
Says what the recorder is doing, and how long it has been going, without changing anything. The elapsed time is counted by AccessCaster from the moment recording started, because the unit does not report a running time of its own; if it was already recording when the app started, it says so rather than giving a number that would be wrong.
Where it records to
The unit records to a memory card or to a USB drive in its second USB port, and needs one of them before it will record at all. Its own internal storage holds the SMART pad sounds and cannot take a recording. The state line at the top of the page names where it will go and how much room is left, given as recording TIME rather than megabytes, worked out from the unit's own data rate, so it answers whether your show fits. It also warns you when a drive is nearly full. The full storage picture, card by card, is on the Device page under Hardware, which is where you go to look at it deliberately.
Recordings on the unit
What is already recorded, newest first, with the date and length. Below the list you can choose one and delete it from the card or drive; you are asked to confirm first, and it cannot be undone. To copy a recording off, take the card or drive to a computer.
The card and drive
Choose the memory card or the USB drive, then Eject it before pulling it out, so nothing is lost mid-write. Once ejected, the same button is named Reconnect and puts it back in use without unplugging it. Erase deletes everything on it, after asking you to confirm. The unit's own internal storage, where the SMART pad sounds live, is never offered here.

CallMe (Alt+5)

Remote guests, over the unit's own wifi or network cable rather than this computer. A guest either has a RODECaster of their own and dials your code, or opens your guest link in a web browser. Calls are announced wherever you are, so the page is for setting up and checking rather than somewhere you have to be when the phone rings.

Call
One line says what is happening: nothing, who is calling, who you are calling, or who you are talking to, and after a call, why it ended. Below it are three buttons. Answer takes a ringing call. The second is named for what it will do: Decline while a call is ringing, Hang up otherwise, and it always turns a ringing call away first, so a second caller arriving mid-conversation is declined rather than the guest you are with being cut off. The third, Extend the call, works in a call's last five minutes; see The one-hour limit. The keyboard shortcuts do the same from any program: Ctrl, Alt and Shift with C answers, or with nothing ringing extends a call that is about to end, and with D declines or hangs up. A call nobody answers stops ringing after two minutes and is announced as missed. Answer says when the call has connected, which is the only real proof: the unit can accept a call whose sound then never starts, and that is reported rather than called a success. While a call is connected, its details follow the buttons, one to a line: how long it has been going, the bitrate, how much audio is being lost, and the jitter.
The one-hour limit
On RODE's free and standard plans a call ends after an hour unless it is extended. Five minutes before, the announcer says so, and Extend the call, or the answer shortcut, gives it another hour. Switch on Extend calls automatically and AccessCaster does it for you and says so.
You need CallMe on a fader to hear a guest
A call can be answered with CallMe on no fader, but then the guest is heard nowhere. The page warns you when that is the case; put CallMe 1 on a fader on Routing, under Faders.
Letting guests call in
The Accept incoming calls switch is the unit's own call hosting. Switched on, the unit is given a code by RODE's service, which another RODECaster dials, and a guest link built from it, which anyone else opens in a browser. Copy the guest link puts it on the clipboard to send. Switching hosting off and on again gets a NEW code, so a link you have already shared stops working; leave it on if you have sent one out. It cannot be changed during a call.
Your plan
Which CallMe plan the unit is on, how many minutes are left, and when they reset, one to a line. The free plan, CallMe Lite, takes one guest at a time with ten hours a month of calls between RODECasters and five of browser calls; a browser call counts against both. RODE's paid plans, bought from your RODE account, add guests and remove the limits.
Call a RODECaster
Type the code shown on the other unit's CallMe screen and press Call, or Enter. A guest link they have sent you works too. The page says straight away whether the call is ringing, or why not: no RODECaster has that code, or they declined. When they answer, the announcer says you are connected. Hang up ends it while it is still ringing, too.
Contacts
Save a name with a RODECaster's code and calls to that unit are announced and shown by the name. Each contact has its own Call, Edit and Delete buttons, and each can go on a keyboard shortcut as "Call" and the name. A unit's code changes whenever its owner switches incoming calls off and on, so if a call to a contact says no RODECaster has that code, ask them for the new one and edit the contact. Contacts are kept on this computer and travel with "Save this app's settings to a file".

Backups (Alt+6)

One list of everything you can put the unit back from, and one button that makes another.

What a backup holds, and where it lives
Channels, processing, routing, the fader layout, the unit's own settings, and every SMART pad with its sound. Two things are left out on purpose: which channels are muted, since putting a mute back could silence somebody at the worst moment, and passwords, so your wifi is never stored and never restored. Underneath, a backup is kept in two places at once when it can be. The unit writes its own complete copy to the card or drive, which goes back in seconds, and this computer keeps a copy of the settings and pads, which survives the drive being pulled, erased or filled. You never have to choose between them: one name covers both. Restoring uses this computer's copy, because asking the unit to load its own can crash it - see Known issues, "The unit can lock up when it restores its own backup". The unit's copy is still made, and is still there if it is ever needed.
Back up the unit now
Opens a dialog that starts on the name box. Type a name and press Back up. Backing up is quick: the unit's own copy and the settings are both a matter of seconds, and the pads add about a second each. Restoring is the slow direction, not this one. Saving to a name you have used before replaces that backup. With no card or drive in the unit there is nowhere for its own copy to go, and the backup is made on this computer alone; the line under the button says which you are getting.
The list, and what each entry says
Every backup you can restore from, whichever half holds it. An entry marked "made on the unit" was taken at the touchscreen or by RODE's own software, so there is no copy of it on this computer - which makes it the one kind that still has to be restored the unit's own way, and the dialog says so before you press anything. One may be marked "as found": that is how your desk was before this app changed anything.
Restore selected backup
Opens a dialog naming the backup, which tells you how it will be put back and roughly how long that takes. Pressing Restore there is the confirmation, and a progress dialog reports each step. Restoring moves the monitor speaker level, so turn speakers down first. What comes back: MIDI, every channel with its levels and, on mics, phantom, polarity, mic type and effects, the full processing chain, all routing including the fader layout, the virtual fader levels and the monitor level, the output mixes and every output's own source levels, what the recorder makes and whether it writes WAV or MP3, auto-mutes, each headphone output's type, the Master Compellor and output delay, and the unit's own settings from the Device page. The SMART pads come back too, each with its play mode, a mixer pad's trigger and which channels a Back Channel pad carries. Mutes are left out on purpose, and passwords are never stored, so wifi is not restored.
Settings the backup does not cover
Your saved settings go back and nothing else is touched. There used to be a "reset the unit to factory first" option here, which cleared everything the backup does not hold before restoring on top. It has been removed: it used the same part of the unit's firmware that crashes it, and that is not a risk worth carrying on an everyday action. Resetting to factory settings is still available on its own, where choosing it is the whole point.
Rename selected backup
Changes the name on this computer, keeping everything in it including the pad sounds. If another backup already uses the name, the dialog stays open and tells you, so nothing is overwritten by accident. A backup made on the unit cannot be renamed: the unit names its own after whichever show was loaded and offers no way to change it.
Delete selected backup
Removes it from both places at once, this computer's copy and the unit's own, so you are not left with a copy on the drive under a name the app no longer lists. You confirm in a dialog that names it first. It cannot be undone, and it changes nothing about how the unit is set up now. The as-found backup asks twice, because it is the only record of how your desk arrived.
The as-found backup
The first time AccessCaster meets a unit it offers to back it up exactly as it was, before the app has changed anything. It is an ordinary backup, just marked as this one, and it is the only record of how your desk was set up before you started. A line under the list says whether you have one, and lets you add the SMART pads to it later if you skipped them at the time.
While a backup or restore is running
The progress dialog cannot be dismissed: there is no Close button and Escape does nothing, because the unit is being read or written and stopping half way would leave it in a state nobody asked for. Close appears as soon as the job finishes, and the result is left on the page after you close it. If the app loses contact with the device service, or the unit reports nothing new for over two minutes, the dialog gives up waiting and lets you close it rather than holding you there.
Start again from factory settings
Puts every setting and every SMART pad back to how the unit left the factory. Your wifi, the language and the analytics answer are not part of it, and neither are the recordings on the drive. The unit backs itself up first, ticked by default, and that backup is the only way back, so leave it ticked unless you are sure. It also rebuilds RODE's eight factory pad sounds from the unit's own firmware, which is the only way to get those back once a pad has been replaced: the app does not carry copies of them. This is not the unit's own factory reset: that one runs a setup wizard afterwards that only works by touchscreen, which is exactly the thing this app exists to avoid.
While a save or restore is running
The progress dialog cannot be dismissed: there is no Close button and Escape does nothing, because the unit is being read or written and stopping half way would leave it in a state nobody asked for. Close appears as soon as the job finishes, and the result is left on the Backups page after you close it. If the app loses contact with the device service, or the unit reports nothing new for over two minutes, the dialog gives up waiting and lets you close it rather than holding you there.
This app's own settings
Last on the page, and the one part of it that is about AccessCaster rather than the unit. Your keyboard shortcuts, announcement preferences, level warnings and CallMe contacts belong to the app, so no backup of the desk carries them and reinstalling would lose them: "Save this app's settings to a file" writes them all into one small file you can keep somewhere safe or copy to another computer, and "Restore these settings" reads one back, with your shortcuts working again straight away and no restart. The file holds nothing about the desk and no access link, so it is safe to email to yourself. Anything in it naming a voice or an audio output this computer has not got is left alone and said out loud rather than applied to nothing. There is also a switch here to start AccessCaster when you sign in to Windows, which is the same thing the installer offered to set up, for your own Windows account only: it starts in the system tray without opening this page, so your shortcut keys work from the moment you sign in.

Device (Alt+7)

The unit's current state, its hardware status, its own settings, and the global switches.

Tabs
This page is a tab strip of eight: Current state, Hardware, Unit settings, Announcements, Keyboard shortcuts, Level warnings, Wireless connections and Remote access. Arrow left and right along the strip, Home and End for the ends, and only the tab you are on is on the page. Opening a tab reads that part of the unit, so the values are always current, and the tab you leave off on is remembered.
Current state
The whole desk in one summary, read from the unit each time you open this tab: what is muted or soloed, every fader level and the monitor level, anything unusual about the routing, and the pad bank. Mute, solo and routing appear only when there is something to report, so a short line means all is well. This is the place to look when something sounds wrong, instead of hunting across the other pages.
Hardware status
Things the touchscreen would otherwise keep to itself: whether a memory card is in and how much room is left, the recorder, the firmware version, and whether a network cable is in. Check for a firmware update asks RODE whether there is a newer version and says what it found; when one is waiting, the summary spoken as the app opens says so too. Installing it is left to the unit's screen (Settings, System, Information) or RODE's app, because an update restarts the unit, and your computer's sound, screen reader included, may be going through it. For wireless mics, see the Wireless connections tab, which has all the radios together. Last on this tab, under its own heading, is a technical section for troubleshooting: whether the unit is found, its hardware id and status bytes, and a low-level list of the USB interfaces seen.
Remote access from another device
The last tab, and the one thing on this page that is about the app rather than the unit. AccessCaster serves its page to your whole network so you can control the desk from a phone in another room, which is genuinely useful and stays. The access link is what stops that being open to anyone else on the network: open it once on the phone and that device is remembered, so reloading and coming back later both work. This computer never needs the link and is unaffected by it. Treat the link like a password, because anyone who has it has full control of the desk. "Make a new link" stops the old one working everywhere you have sent it, which is what you want if you shared it at a venue. Two honest limits: the link can only be read on this computer, not from the phone itself, and it travels unencrypted, so it protects you from a stranger finding the address rather than from someone who can already read your network's traffic.
Unit settings
The settings the unit otherwise offers only on its touchscreen: screen brightness and whether the unit sets it itself, how long before the screen dims, how brightly the buttons are lit when active and when not, the meter style, haptic feedback, whether the record button pauses or stops, the listen mode, and the monitor output's fixed level. Listen mode is worth a word: pre-fader listen hears a soloed channel at its own level whatever its fader is doing, which is what you want for checking a mic that is currently faded down; after-fader listen hears it as the fader has it set, so a fader all the way down means silence. Then the clock settings (24-hour, and whether the time shows on the unit's screen), and three lockouts that can lock the unit's physical buttons or silence every headphone or line output. The time itself, the timezone and daylight saving are not here: the unit sets its own clock over the network, and it only applies a timezone or daylight saving change made on its own touchscreen. They are read from the unit each time you open the tab, and a backup holds them and puts them back. They work on both units. Last on the tab is the MIDI switch: turning it on lets an external MIDI app or controller handle mute, faders, listen and record over MIDI, without RODE Central. That one works on both units.
Announcements
Headed "Announce Interactions on Mixer" once you are on the tab. It speaks each physical control aloud as you use it, so you know what it did without the touchscreen. Mute and solo say which channel and whether it is now on or off, and catch changes made anywhere, not just here. Bank changes are announced, as is what the encoder is set to when you press and release it, and pressing a channel button says what the encoder will then adjust; press and HOLD the encoder and the unit mutes the selected channel, which you hear as a mute rather than as a change of selection. A second switch adds levels, spoken as you move them: whatever you turn is announced by name, so the encoder set to a channel gives you that channel's gain in decibels rather than the monitor, each update interrupts the last so it keeps up with you, and the closing value is confirmed by name when you stop. A muted channel is announced as muted rather than with a level. The volume slider sets how loud announcements are, for when the announcer talks over what you are listening to while riding the faders; it affects these announcements only, never your Windows volume or the unit's audio, and it is remembered between sessions. You can also choose which installed speech VOICE reads them, and which audio OUTPUT they come out of. Both apply to these announcements only: your screen reader keeps its own voice and its own output, the Windows default output is not moved, and no other program is affected. The output choice matters because the default output here is usually the RODECaster itself, so announcements arrive on USB 1 and vanish when you solo another channel or mute USB 1, as well as landing in your recording; sending them to your computer's own speakers or headphones instead keeps them audible and out of the mix. Changing either one speaks a sample through it, so you hear what you have picked, and both are remembered. A button beside them reads both lists again, for when you have plugged headphones in or installed a new voice since the page loaded.
Speak the desk's state when the app opens
On the same tab. When the app opens it tells you what is WRONG with the desk and only that: anything muted or soloed, a voice-effect pad left switched on, a recording already running, a drive nearly full, or an output not on its usual mix. On a desk with nothing to report it is one sentence saying so. The full picture, every fader level included, stays on the Device page under Current state.
Play a tone for mute instead of speaking it
Also on that tab, and off unless you turn it on. A mute is usually pressed in the middle of something, and a spoken sentence finishes after the moment it was describing, so a short cue says it faster: two notes falling means muted, two notes rising means unmuted. Turning it on plays both, in that order, so you hear which is which. What you give up is the channel name, which a cue cannot carry, so leave it off if you need to know WHICH channel was muted rather than that one was.
Keyboard shortcuts
Shortcuts that work anywhere in Windows, whatever program you are in, and even with this window closed, for as long as AccessCaster is running. Every shortcut is held on the same keys, Ctrl, Alt and Shift to begin with, so there is only ever one thing to hold and the key you press decides what happens. "Change the keys to hold" moves every shortcut to different ones at once. Your shortcuts are one list: arrow up and down through it, and each one says what it does and which key it is on. Enter opens it for editing (which key to press, whether it is on, and whether it says what it did), and nothing changes until you press Save. Delete removes it, after asking. The Applications key or Shift+F10 opens a menu that also turns it off or on, or switches its speech, in one step; with a mouse or a touch screen, click the shortcut instead. The list is yours to build: Add a shortcut offers a catalogue of over 100 commands, sorted into kinds (channels, levels, recording, CallMe, pads, outputs, mixes, the unit and the app itself), so anything you can do on a page you can put on a key, and you choose its key there too. A new install starts with seven of its own: turn announcements off and on, mute every mic and open them again (the cough key), start or stop a recording, hear what the unit is doing, bring this window to the front, answer a CallMe call or extend one about to end (C), and decline one or hang up (D), plus the SMART pad keys described below. "Use the SMART pad keys" adds or removes the pad keys as a set, and "Restore default shortcuts" puts the whole list back as a new install has it, after asking, because anything you have added or changed is lost. To copy your shortcuts to another computer, save this app's settings on the Backups page. A shortcut has to include Ctrl, Alt or Windows, so it cannot take a plain key away from your screen reader; if another program already owns the combination you pick, this page says so rather than leaving you with a key that does nothing. The cough key opens again only the mics it muted, so it never puts a channel live that you had shut yourself. Levels come in pairs, up and down, because a key cannot ask how far. Nothing that cannot be undone is offered at all. Each shortcut normally speaks what it did, because when you press one you will be looking at something else, and each can be told to stay quiet instead. The announcements one is always silent when it turns announcements OFF: the reason to reach for it is usually that announcements are going into a recording, and saying so into that recording would be the very thing you were stopping. Anything that FAILS speaks whatever you have set. There is one switch at the top that releases the lot. Every kind of pad can be put on a key, sound pads included.
Playing pads from the keyboard
The pad set is one row of keys you can find by touch, on Ctrl, Alt and Shift like everything else. The NUMBER KEYS 1 to 8 play the pads of the bank the unit is on, so eight keys reach every pad on the unit rather than only the first eight; on a RODECaster Duo it is 1 to 6, because a Duo has six pads in a bank. DASH and EQUALS, at the end of the same row, step down and up a bank and say which bank you have landed on, including when you are already at the first or last. P says what is playing, how far through it is and how long is left. You can also put ONE particular pad on a key, from the Add list, and that one stays with that pad wherever the unit is: useful for a jingle you always want under the same finger. The number keys are the opposite, and both are worth having. These keys do NOT speak what they did, unlike the rest, because the pad itself is the feedback and it is usually going out on air. You can turn speech on for any of them. A pad still fails out loud. What a SECOND press does is the pad's own setting, made on the SMART pads page or on the unit, not something this app decides: Toggle stops the sound, One Shot starts it again from the beginning, and Hold plays for as long as you leave it, until you press the key again. A voice effect or a mixer pad switches on and off instead, and stays on until you press again, exactly as it does on the unit. The first time you play a sound pad in a session the app switches the unit's MIDI control on and says so once. That is the only way to make the unit play a sound, and it is the same switch as the one on the Device page, so you can turn it off there when you are done. Restoring a backup can switch it off again, and the next pad you press turns it back on.
Level warnings
The watch that speaks up while you work, and the Windows level it depends on. If you want to check one channel before a take, use "Check this channel's level" on the Channels page instead: this tab watches a whole stream. Pick which of the unit's audio streams to watch, set the Windows recording level for it, which is a hidden multiplier on everything measured, then turn on a background watch that stays quiet unless it has news: it can say when the stream clips, and when it goes silent or comes back, with how long a gap counts as silence. It can also speak the level on a timer, which is off by default and is meant for setting gains rather than for use during a recording. Two things to know. It reads the unit as a USB AUDIO DEVICE, through Windows, not over the control connection, so it is not the unit's own meter and will not match it exactly. And it is the only part of this app that does not work over Remote Desktop: Windows gives each session its own audio devices, so from a remote session the unit's streams are not there to read. The page says so when that happens.
Wireless connections
Every radio link the unit can make, in one place. "Scan for networks" takes a few seconds and then offers what it found in a list, saying for each whether it needs a password and which one the unit is already set to; picking one fills in the network name below, which you can also just type. The unit reports ten networks at most and never reports hidden ones, so typing the name is sometimes the only way. Joining takes up to 45 seconds and can only be done at the computer the unit is plugged into. Bluetooth has one switch, which makes the unit discoverable so you can pair from the phone's own Bluetooth screen; the unit stops being discoverable again after a few minutes. Then the wireless mics the unit can see and their battery levels, and under those, what the unit reports: the network it is on, its address, what a scan last found, and which Bluetooth devices are paired. Two things are missing here: searching for Bluetooth devices, and starting a pairing from this end. A fixed IP address is not offered either, and that one is the unit's own limitation: it reports its address but gives nothing to set it with. The Bluetooth CHANNEL, meaning what you hear from a paired phone, is on the Channels page, and what the phone hears back from the desk is the Bluetooth output on the Routing page.

The app itself: the tray icon and this window

AccessCaster is two things: a program that sits in the notification area, and this page, which is how you drive it. Closing this window does NOT close the app, and that is the point: the program carries on running, your keyboard shortcuts carry on working from whatever you are doing, and the announcer carries on telling you what the unit is up to. It is also why one window is all there is; opening the app again brings this one back rather than starting a second copy.

Reach the icon with the Windows key and B, then arrow to AccessCaster and press the applications key or Enter. Its menu holds four things: Open AccessCaster, which brings this page back, Announcer, which switches spoken announcements off and on and is ticked while they are on, Diagnostic log, which opens the app's own record of what it sent to the unit, and Quit, which stops the program properly, shortcut keys and all.

If the program stops while this window is open, because it was quit, updated or crashed, the window tells you so in a dialog after about twelve seconds, since nothing on the page can work without it. When the program comes back, the window says so; if it came back as a newer version, the window reloads itself so you are using the new page.

If the unit stops responding

Every so often the RØDECaster drops off the USB connection by itself. It is a fault in the unit rather than in this app, nothing is lost when it happens, and it is most likely just after a factory reset, which is the moment the unit rebuilds its USB connection from scratch.

What it looks like
Every control stops working and the app says it cannot find the unit, or that it is half connected. Your audio may carry on regardless, because the desk keeps mixing whatever it is doing and a second computer on the other USB socket has its own connection, so silence is not the signal to watch for. The desk itself is fine; it is only the connection to this computer that has gone.
What to do first
Unplug the USB cable in the socket nearest the power inlet, wait a few seconds, and plug it back in. That is the socket this app talks to the unit over, and the other one carries audio only. Do not skip the wait: putting it straight back in is what fails most often.
If that does not work
Try it once or twice more. A replug is simply another attempt at the same thing, and it can take more than one. Give it a good few seconds out each time.
If it still will not appear
This is RØDE's own procedure for a unit that will not come back. Disconnect the power, press and hold the red button on the back for sixty seconds, then reconnect the power supply on its own, with no other cables, and press the red button to switch the unit on. Add the rest of your cables once it has started up.
What it does not affect
Your settings, your pads and your recordings. Nothing is lost by a dropout, and nothing needs restoring afterwards. If it happened during a factory reset, the reset itself almost certainly went through, and the list on the Backups page will show it once the unit is back.

Known issues

Faults I know about and have measured, so you can recognise one rather than wonder whether it is something you have done. All of these are the unit's own behaviour rather than something this app does to it.

The unit can drop off the USB connection when you reset it

About one time in five, a reset ends with the RØDECaster leaving the USB connection and not coming back on its own. I have reproduced it on my own Pro 2, and the reset command by itself is enough: nothing else has to be going on, no other software has to be running, and it happens just the same with nothing plugged into the unit's second USB socket. It is not this app holding the connection open, which was the obvious suspect and was ruled out with a test that holds nothing open at all.

What you will see
Every control stops working and the app says it cannot find the unit, or that it is half connected. Your audio may carry on regardless, so silence is not the signal to watch for.
What to do
Unplug the USB cable in the socket nearest the power inlet, wait a few seconds, and plug it back in. The Help tab, under "If the unit stops responding", has the rest of it, including RØDE's own procedure for a unit that will not come back at all.
What it costs you
Nothing. The reset itself goes through, and no settings, pads or recordings are lost by the drop. The list on the Backups page will show it once the unit is back.

The unit can lock up when it restores its own backup, so this app no longer asks it to

Measured across a whole day on my own Pro 2, on 21st September 2026, with the app logging every command it sent. Restoring from the unit's own copy of a backup crashed it five times in thirty-one attempts, on three different backups. Restoring from this computer's copy did not fail once in nine attempts, including four long ones back to back.

So the Restore button now always uses this computer's copy. Backing up has not changed: the unit still makes its own copy alongside, because backing up has never caused this, and that copy is there if it is ever needed. Only the way a backup is read back has changed. It takes about two minutes instead of a few seconds, and it restores a little less, which is a price worth paying to not have this happen to you.

This is the unit's own fault, not something this app does wrong. It crashes inside its own firmware while it reloads its settings, and once it did that with a tone left looping on the headphone output. Nothing the app sends is incorrect, and no change to the app prevents it, which is why avoiding it is the only fix available. I am still working on it, and if a way is found to do it safely the faster route will come back.

Why it matters more than it sounds
If your screen reader plays through the RODECaster, a unit that stops responding takes your speech with it until it is back. That is the reason this was worth changing rather than warning about.
What it looks like
Audio stops and every control stops answering. Once, it also left a single tone repeating on the headphones, so that can happen but do not wait for it. Windows may still list the unit for a minute afterwards, so the unit disappearing from your sound settings is not the first sign.
What to do if it happens
Unplugging the USB cable will probably not be enough, because the unit itself has stopped rather than the connection. Pull the power lead, wait half a minute, and plug it back in.
What it costs you
Nothing is lost. The restore goes through before the unit stops answering, and the backups on the drive are untouched. Check your monitor speaker level when it comes back, because a restore moves it.
Where it can still happen
Two places, both of which you have to choose deliberately. Resetting the unit to factory settings uses the same part of its firmware. And a backup the unit made on its own, with no copy on this computer beside it, can only be restored the unit's own way; the Restore dialog tells you when that is the case.

Voice effect pads that do nothing, after a reset from somewhere else

When the unit loads its default show, the six factory voice effect pads come back with the right name, type and colour, but the effects themselves do not come back with them. The pads read exactly as they should, and the unit accepts the press and shows the pad as engaged, but your voice is unchanged.

When it happens
Only when the desk is reset by something other than this app: its own touchscreen, RØDE's software, or another tool. "Start again from factory settings" on the Backups page rebuilds the six effects as part of the reset, so you will not meet it there.
How to recognise it
The SMART pads page says so in as many words: six voice-effect pads have no effect to play, so they do nothing. Nothing on the unit's own screen shows the fault at all, and a keyboard shortcut on one of those pads still reports the effect as on, because the press is accepted; there is simply nothing behind it to play.
The fix
On the SMART pads page, Maintenance tab, choose "Restore the factory voice effects, Censor and Ducking". It rebuilds those six, and the Censor and Ducking pads with them, because all eight can be rebuilt from settings alone with no sound file needed. That means pads 9 to 16 are cleared and recreated, so move anything of your own off those positions first. Nothing past pad 16 is touched. It takes two or three minutes, and it is worth an ear-check on each one afterwards.
